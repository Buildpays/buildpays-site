1. Who we are

PayKicker is software operated by PAYKICKER PTY LTD (ABN 91 700 949 944), a company based in Melbourne, Victoria. We provide rostering, site induction, dayworks docket and payroll-preparation software to Australian construction subcontractors.

We handle personal information in accordance with the Australian Privacy Principles in the Privacy Act 1988 (Cth).

2. Two different roles — this matters

We hold personal information in two distinct capacities, and your rights differ depending on which one applies to you.

a. Information about our own customers

When a construction business subscribes to PayKicker, we collect information about that business and the people who administer the account. We decide how that information is handled, and this policy governs it directly.

b. Worker information we hold for an employer

Most of the personal information in PayKicker is about workers, and we hold it on behalf of their employer. The employer decides what is collected, who inside their business can see it, and how long it is kept. We process it on their instructions and do not use it for our own purposes.

If you are a worker and you want to see, correct or delete your information, the fastest path is to ask your employer — they control the record. You can also contact us and we will help, but where the law allows we will refer your request to your employer rather than act on their records without them.

Employers can also add their own questions to onboarding forms. When an employer adds a question that asks for something sensitive, our software warns them on screen — but it does not block them, because the forms belong to the employer. What an employer chooses to ask for is their responsibility, not ours.

3. What we collect

From businesses that subscribe

  • Business name, ABN, address and contact details
  • Names, work email addresses, phone numbers and roles of account administrators
  • Subscription and billing records. We do not store card numbers — card details are entered directly with our payment processor and we never see them
  • A log of significant actions taken in the account (who approved a timesheet, who ran a pay export, who changed a setting) for audit and dispute resolution

From workers, on behalf of their employer

  • Name, employee/payroll card number, email address, phone number
  • Employment classification and employment type (daily hire, casual, apprentice, salaried)
  • Date of birth, home address, and emergency contact details
  • Licences, tickets and qualifications — card numbers, issue and expiry dates, and photographs of the cards themselves
  • Driver licence details, where the employer enables the vehicle policy form
  • Signatures captured when acknowledging a Safe Work Method Statement or signing a dayworks docket, stored as images
  • Hours worked, dates, sites and projects worked on, and leave requests
  • Answers to any additional questions the employer adds to their onboarding forms

From clients of our customers

Where a builder or head contractor signs a dayworks docket, we record the signatory's name, the time of signing and their signature.

From visitors to this website

See section 10.

4. Sensitive information

Some of what PayKicker holds is sensitive information under the Privacy Act, which carries stricter obligations. Specifically:

  • Health information — medical conditions or allergies a worker declares on an induction form, so that a site can respond appropriately in an emergency.
  • Information about racial or ethnic origin — whether a worker identifies as Aboriginal or Torres Strait Islander, along with gender, veteran status and language spoken at home, where an employer runs a diversity survey.

Diversity survey questions are optional and clearly marked as optional in the app. A worker may skip them or answer "prefer not to say" without consequence. We collect sensitive information only where the worker provides it and the employer has a legitimate reason for asking — workplace health and safety, or reporting obligations.

Sensitive information is stored under the same access controls as everything else and is never used for marketing, profiling, or any purpose beyond the one it was collected for.

5. What we never collect

PayKicker does not ask for tax file numbers, bank account or BSB details, or superannuation fund information.

Those details belong in your payroll system, which lodges tax declarations to the ATO under Single Touch Payroll and performs the stapled-super fund lookup — neither of which PayKicker does. Collecting them here would mean holding the most sensitive data a worker has, for no purpose, while creating a breach risk with no offsetting benefit.

If anyone asks you for your tax file number or bank details inside PayKicker, or by text message claiming to be from us, it is not us. Please report it to operations@paykicker.com.au.

6. How we use personal information

We use it only to deliver the service:

  • Rostering crews, recording attendance and calculating hours under an enterprise agreement
  • Producing timesheets and payroll files for the employer's payroll system
  • Tracking induction status, ticket expiry and SWMS acknowledgement so that unqualified or un-inducted workers are not rostered onto a site
  • Generating and signing dayworks dockets and client reports
  • Sending operational messages — roster changes, docket signature requests, login codes
  • Account administration, billing and support
  • Keeping the service secure, diagnosing faults and meeting our legal obligations

We do not sell personal information. We do not share it for advertising. We do not use worker data to train machine learning models.

8. Overseas disclosure

Your core records — worker details, timesheets, dockets, tickets and signatures — are stored in Australia, in a Sydney data centre, and that is where they stay. Our own backup copies of that data are kept for 30 days in our hosting provider's Oceania storage region, which spans Australia and New Zealand.

Some information is handled overseas by the providers in the table above, for as long as it takes to provide the service and no longer: email addresses and message content when we send an email; billing details when you pay; technical error reports with personal details removed before they leave; website analytics; an address sent for lookup; the photo of a ticket, licence or fare receipt while it is being read; and the counts and name-free warning text in a payroll pre-flight brief. The countries are the United States and the European Union, and New Zealand for the backup copies described above. Each provider is bound by contract to use the information only to provide its service to us and not for its own purposes, and we take the steps Australian Privacy Principle 8 requires to ensure it handles the information consistently with the Australian Privacy Principles. Where a provider keeps a copy briefly for abuse monitoring, it is deleted within 30 days.

9. Payroll connections

Where a customer connects their payroll system, we read the information needed to prepare pay correctly — employee records and payroll card numbers, payroll category configuration, and job and cost-centre codes — and we write approved timesheets back.

We send hours, never dollars. All pay rates, tax, superannuation and Single Touch Payroll reporting remain in the payroll system. We do not store payroll system passwords: connections are authorised by the customer through their payroll provider's own secure sign-in, and can be disconnected by the customer at any time.

10. Website, cookies and analytics

This website uses Google Analytics 4 and Cloudflare analytics to understand how many people visit and which pages are useful. These set cookies or similar identifiers and record your approximate location (from your IP address), device and browser type, the pages you view, and how you arrived — including any campaign tags in the link you followed.

If you submit the enquiry form, we collect the name, email address, phone number and message you provide, along with any campaign tags, so we can respond and understand which enquiries came from which source. That information is delivered to operations@paykicker.com.au.

This website also carries advertising measurement pixels from Google Ads, Meta (Facebook and Instagram), LinkedIn and TikTok. When you visit, each pixel may set a cookie and send that platform the page you viewed, your IP address, browser details and, if you arrived from one of our ads on that platform, the click identifier in the link. If you submit the enquiry form, the pixel also reports that an enquiry was made, without your name, email address or message. We use this to measure whether our advertising is reaching the right businesses, and to show our ads to people who have visited this site. Each platform may match that data to an account you hold with it and use it under its own privacy policy: Google, Meta, LinkedIn, TikTok. We do not upload your enquiry details to any of these platforms.

You can block cookies in your browser settings, install Google's opt-out add-on, or manage ad personalisation in your Meta, LinkedIn and TikTok settings. The website works normally either way.

The PayKicker application itself does not use advertising or tracking cookies. It stores only what is needed to keep you signed in.

11. How we protect information

  • Access boundaries are enforced in the database, not just hidden in the interface. Each business can read only its own records. This is enforced on every request.
  • Passwordless sign-in. There are no passwords to steal or reuse — access is by a single-use code sent to your email address.
  • Encryption in transit for all traffic, and encryption at rest for the database and stored files.
  • Least privilege. Our own support staff have read-only access to customer data and cannot alter it. Payroll and pricing controls are locked at the database level.
  • Personal information is stripped from error reports before they are sent to our monitoring provider, including anything resembling an email address, phone number, address or identity number.
  • An audit log records significant account actions.
  • Uploaded files — ticket photographs, signatures, payroll exports — are held in private storage reachable only through short-lived, single-purpose links.

No system is perfectly secure. We keep our software patched, limit what we collect in the first place, and design so that a mistake in one place does not expose everything.

12. How long we keep information

We keep worker and payroll records for as long as the employer's account is active, because employment and pay records must be retained under the Fair Work Act 2009 — generally for seven years.

Some things go sooner, automatically, on a nightly schedule: a docket signing link is purged 30 days after it expires; the text of an SMS we sent is blanked after 90 days and the record of it deleted after 12 months; a worker's onboarding answers and the photographs of their tickets and licences are deleted 12 months after their employment ends (the record of the ticket — its type, number and expiry — stays with the employment record); safety documents and their signatures are deleted two years after the project they belong to closes; and audit-log entries are deleted after seven years.

When a customer's subscription ends, their administrators keep read access for 90 days to download their records, with a reminder at day 60. At day 90 the account and everything in it is deleted, unless we are required to keep it longer by law. From then on the employer holds the records it downloaded and the retention obligations under the Fair Work Act are met from that copy. Customers can ask us to delete specific records sooner. Backup copies are kept for 7 days by our database provider and for 30 days in our own backup store, then overwritten — so a deleted record can persist in a backup for up to 30 days and no longer.

13. Access, correction and deletion

You can ask us:

  • what personal information we hold about you
  • for a copy of it
  • to correct anything inaccurate, out of date or incomplete
  • to delete it, where we are not required to keep it

Email operations@paykicker.com.au. We will respond within 30 days. There is no charge. We may need to verify your identity first, and — as explained in section 2 — if the records belong to your employer we will usually direct your request to them.

If we refuse a request, we will tell you why in writing and how to complain about it.

14. Data breaches

If a data breach occurs that is likely to result in serious harm, we will notify affected individuals and the Office of the Australian Information Commissioner as required by the Notifiable Data Breaches scheme. We will also promptly notify any affected customer, so they can meet their own obligations to their workers.

15. Complaints

If you think we have mishandled your personal information, please tell us first — email operations@paykicker.com.au with "Privacy complaint" in the subject line. We will acknowledge within 5 business days and respond within 30 days.

If you are not satisfied with our response, you can complain to the Office of the Australian Information Commissioner: oaic.gov.au · 1300 363 992 · GPO Box 5218, Sydney NSW 2001.

16. Changes to this policy

We update this policy when the product or our providers change. The effective date at the top always shows the current version. If a change materially affects how we handle your information, we will notify account holders by email before it takes effect.
