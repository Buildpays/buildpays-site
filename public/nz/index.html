Supplier invoices, live in New Zealand now.

The same supplier invoices Australian businesses use, set to New Zealand: GST at 15%, GST numbers, New Zealand bank accounts and your file's own tax codes.

What it does

  • Send it in: forward bills to your own invoices address, or upload a PDF or photo.
  • Two readers read every figure. Where they disagree, the figure is outlined for you to confirm against the bill.
  • Tax invoice or taxable supply information: since 1 April 2023 IRD calls the GST record taxable supply information. PayKicker reads a bill headed either way.
  • GST at 15% split from the no-GST lines, onto your file's own codes. Xero NZ: “15% GST on Expenses” and “No GST”. MYOB Business NZ: S15 and E.
  • Coded and approved, then sent to Xero or MYOB as a bill ready to pay.
  • No jobs in your file? Bills go through without one. Once the file has a job, every bill needs one.

What it checks first

  • The supplier, matched in Xero NZ by GST number and name. A MYOB Business NZ supplier card has no GST number, so in MYOB the match is by name.
  • The GST number on the bill, against the IRD check digit. PayKicker checks the format; it does not look the number up on a register.
  • Duplicates: the same supplier, number and total as a bill already in PayKicker, Xero or MYOB.
  • The bank account: the New Zealand account number on the bill (15 or 16 digits) is compared with the one your Xero or MYOB holds for that supplier. If they differ, the bill says not to pay until you have confirmed it with the supplier.
  • The GST maths: subtotal plus GST equals the total, and the lines' tax codes give the GST printed on the bill.

Questions.

Do you charge New Zealand GST?

No. PayKicker Pty Ltd is an Australian company. You give your GST number or NZBN when you sign up, and with it New Zealand law treats the service as supplied outside New Zealand, so no New Zealand GST is added (GST Act 1985, s 8BB(2)). No Australian GST is charged either: each monthly invoice says GST is not charged because the service is supplied to a GST-registered business outside Australia. The price you see is the price you pay.

Do I need a GST number?

Yes, or your NZBN. PayKicker sells to GST-registered businesses in New Zealand only, and the sign-up does not finish without one. The number is checked for format: the IRD check digit for a GST number, the check digit for an NZBN. It is not looked up on a register.

Does it work with MYOB Business NZ?

Yes. PayKicker has been tested on a MYOB Business NZ file: bills go in as purchase bills on S15 and E. A MYOB Business NZ supplier card has no GST number, so suppliers are matched there by name. PayKicker has not yet been tested on an AccountRight NZ file; if you use AccountRight, email support@paykicker.com.au before you start.

What about zero-rated and no-GST lines?

A bill with 15% lines and no-GST lines is split into a 15% part and a no-GST part that add up to the bill, each on its own tax code. In Xero NZ the no-GST part goes on No GST, because Xero NZ keeps Zero Rated for sales. In MYOB Business NZ it goes on E. PayKicker checks that the codes give the GST printed on the bill.

Can I use it with Shopify, Square or Uber Eats?

Shopify sales sync for New Zealand stores is opening soon: it is built and tested, and waits on the Shopify App Store review. Square is not available in New Zealand. Uber Eats and DoorDash payouts are not available in New Zealand yet.

Which currency am I billed in?

New Zealand dollars, once a month, by card through Stripe. The numbers are the same as the Australian prices, with no GST added: NZ$9.99, NZ$29.99, NZ$69.99 or NZ$149, for the band your month lands in. PayKicker Pty Ltd, an Australian company, issues the bill.

Where is my data kept?

The main store of your data is in Australia, in a Sydney data centre. PayKicker's own encrypted backup copies are kept for 30 days in its hosting provider's Oceania storage region, which spans Australia and New Zealand. Some information, such as email and card payments, is handled overseas by the providers listed in the privacy policy.
