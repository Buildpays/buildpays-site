Every line of the statement, on its own account.

The platform pays you less than you sold, and the gap is commission, fees, refunds and offers. PayKicker puts each one where your accountant expects it, against a clearing account for each platform.

What it posts

  • One entry per payout, dated the payout date. In MYOB a payout with both sales and fee lines goes as two journals the same day, so each side is reported correctly on your BAS.
  • Commission and fees on your fees account; ads the platform charges on your marketing account.
  • Offers you fund, refunds, error charges and adjustments off your sales, at your sales tax code.
  • Tips on a tips account: owed to staff, not income.
  • A clearing account per platform, so Uber Eats and DoorDash each clear to zero.

What it checks first

  • GST on the platform's fees is claimed only once you tick that you hold its tax invoice. Until then the fees post GST-free and the payout says so.
  • A week that nets below zero posts to the clearing account with no bank line; Uber's Remaining Balance on the next payout clears it.
  • A line it can't place, such as a Reversal or Other payments, waits until you choose once: sales, fee or clearing. PayKicker remembers.
  • Figures that don't add up to the statement's own payout are held as Needs a look, with the reason. PayKicker never posts a balancing line.
  • Never twice: before writing, PayKicker looks for the entry in your file, so a retry finishes the job instead of doubling it.

Questions.

Can I claim GST on Uber Eats and DoorDash commission?

Only when you hold the platform's tax invoice for its fees, with its ABN and the GST shown. Until you tick that you hold it, PayKicker posts the fees GST-free and says so on every payout. Once ticked, the GST is claimed at your fees tax code, and when the statement shows the GST on the fees PayKicker checks the two agree; if they don't, the payout waits for a look. Your accountant has the final word on what you claim.

My till already records Uber Eats or DoorDash orders. Will they be counted twice?

No. You tell PayKicker once, per platform, that your till already records the orders. The sales then come into your books from the till as they do today, and PayKicker posts only the payout against that platform's clearing account. With Square connected, PayKicker suggests the answer from the payment names it sees, and you confirm it.

What about DoorDash's monthly statement?

The monthly statement is a PDF, and PayKicker doesn't read it. Use the CSV reports instead: in the DoorDash Merchant Portal, Reports, Create report, Financials, then Payouts or Transactions breakdown. DoorDash emails you when the report is ready and keeps it for 7 days. Download it and upload it, or forward the email with the CSV attached.

How are tips handled?

Tips are owed to your staff, so they are not income. They post to a tips account you choose, with no GST, and stay there until you pay them out.

What does it cost?

Delivery payouts are part of the Sales sync and cost nothing extra: free up to 60 orders a month, $9.99 up to 500 and $29.99 unlimited, per month, inc GST. When PayKicker posts your delivery sales too, those orders count toward the same monthly total; when your till records them, they are already counted there.

Do I need Square?

No. A café that takes delivery orders on the platforms' own tablets signs up for the Sales sync and connects MYOB or Xero; PayKicker then posts the delivery sales, with their GST, as well as each payout.
