2. What the service is

PayKicker is subscription software for Australian construction subcontractors. Depending on the modules you take, it provides rostering, site inductions and SWMS acknowledgement, worker onboarding and ticket records, timesheets and approval workflow, dayworks dockets with electronic signatures, client cost reports, and preparation of payroll files for your payroll system.

We may improve, change or remove features over time. If we remove or materially reduce a feature you rely on, we will give you at least 30 days' notice, and you may cancel under section 12 if the change does not suit you.

3. Pilot period

PayKicker has no self-serve trial. Before you rely on it for pay, we set you up and run a pilot period alongside your existing process, at no charge, until the payroll file PayKicker produces matches what you would otherwise have paid.

Setup and configuration of your enterprise agreement rules is included in your subscription. Billing starts when the pilot ends and you begin using PayKicker for live pay runs, unless we agree otherwise in writing.

Do not skip the reconciliation. The pilot exists so that a difference between PayKicker and your current process is found by a bookkeeper rather than by an underpaid worker. We will tell you when the numbers agree. Going live before then is at your own risk.

4. Your responsibility for pay

This section matters more than any other in this agreement.

You are the employer. You remain solely responsible for paying your workers correctly and on time, and for complying with the Fair Work Act 2009, your enterprise agreement, any applicable modern award, and all other laws that apply to you. Using PayKicker does not transfer any of that to us.

You acknowledge that:

  • PayKicker does not provide legal or industrial relations advice. It applies pay rules as configured. It is not a substitute for advice from a qualified adviser, and nothing in the product should be read as a legal opinion about your agreement.
  • You must verify the rules against your own agreement. Enterprise agreements differ, are amended, and are open to interpretation. We configure PayKicker to your agreement as we understand it, and you are responsible for checking that what it produces is what your agreement requires.
  • PayKicker calculates hours, not money. It sends quantities — hours and units — to your payroll system. Every pay rate, loading and dollar amount lives in your payroll system, not in PayKicker. If a rate or a payroll category in that system is set up incorrectly, the file will import cleanly and the pay will be wrong.
  • You must keep your payroll system configured correctly. We will tell you what configuration PayKicker requires. Keeping it that way is your responsibility, and you must tell us if it changes.
  • You must review each pay run before you pay it. PayKicker flags unusual variances, but a warning is a prompt to check, not a guarantee that everything unflagged is correct.

You are responsible for the accuracy of what you and your workers enter — hours, sites, classifications, allowances, approvals and payroll settings.

You approve every pay run

PayKicker does not pay anybody. It prepares figures. You review them, you approve them, and you pay them through your own payroll system. Approving a pay run is the point at which you accept that the figures are correct for your workers and your agreement.

Telling us about a pay error — within 24 hours

If you become aware of an error in anything PayKicker has calculated, exported or reported — or of any discrepancy between a PayKicker figure and what you actually paid — you must tell us as soon as you become aware of it, and in any event within 24 hours, at operations@paykicker.com.au.

This is not a formality, and it is not about blame. A calculation issue does not affect one payslip — it repeats in every pay run until someone stops it, across your whole crew. Told the day you notice, it is one correction. Told three months later, it is three months of the same error in everyone's pay, back-payment for all of them, and a far worse conversation with your workers.

The single most useful thing you can do for your own exposure is tell us the moment something looks wrong — even if you are not sure it is.

We are not liable for any loss that we could reasonably have prevented or reduced had you told us promptly, as required by this section.
