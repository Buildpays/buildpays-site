How to get Square sales into MYOB

Square knows every sale. MYOB needs to know them too, in a shape your BAS and your bank reconciliation can use: sales by GST code, the card money Square is holding, the cash, and what Square kept when it paid you. Here is how to do it by hand, where it usually goes wrong, and how to have it posted every night.

Why MYOB needs a link to Square

Square keeps your sales and MYOB keeps your books, and nothing moves between them unless something connects them. MYOB's own help connects Square through a partner app rather than a built-in feed. If your Square account isn't connected to MYOB, someone has to enter every day's takings from Square's reports.

The sales are only half of it. Square takes its processing fees out before it transfers your money, so the deposit that reaches the bank is the card takings less the fees, and it lands later than the sale. MYOB needs both sides, the day's sales and the payout, or the bank never reconciles.

The manual way: a daily entry, a clearing account and the payouts

  1. Set up the accounts once. A bank-type account for the card money Square is holding (often called Square Balance or Square clearing), an income account for sales and one for card surcharges, liability accounts for tips owed to staff and for gift cards sold, and an expense account for Square's fees. Two shops? A job for each.
  2. Each day, open Square's sales summary for each shop. You need the sales with GST, the GST-free sales, surcharges, tips, gift cards sold and redeemed, refunds, and how it was paid: card, cash, gift card.
  3. Enter one tax-inclusive entry for the day. One way is a sales invoice to a customer called Square, one per shop: GST sales on the GST code, GST-free sales on FRE, surcharges on their income account, tips and gift cards sold on N-T to their liability accounts, refunds and gift cards redeemed as negative lines, every line on the shop's job.
  4. Record how it was paid. The card total into the Square clearing account, the cash into your cash account.
  5. When Square pays out, settle it. Square's fees come out of the clearing account to the fees account, on the GST code; the deposit is a transfer from the clearing account to your bank.
  6. Check the clearing account. It should hold only card takings Square has not paid out yet. If it drifts, a day or a payout is missing or wrong.

What goes wrong

  • Fees. Entering the bank deposit as the day's sales understates your sales and loses the fees, and the GST on them. Square charges its fees on the whole of each transaction, GST and tip included, and takes them out before it pays you, so the deposit is never the sales figure.
  • Refunds. A refund belongs to the day it was given, carrying the GST of what was refunded. The ATO treats a refund for a returned sale as an adjustment in the period you become aware of it, so last week's figures stay as they were. Putting every refund on the GST code overstates the GST credit when the item was GST-free.
  • Tips. Entered as sales, they inflate your income and your GST. The ATO's view is that a voluntary tip passed on to staff is not part of the price of the sale, so it belongs in a liability until it is paid out to staff.
  • GST. One sales line on the GST code for a day that included GST-free items overstates the GST you owe. Square records the tax on each item; the entry needs the GST and GST-free totals apart. (The same split for supplier bills: Invoices with GST and GST-free items.)
  • Gift cards. Selling a gift card is not a sale for GST: the ATO treats a card that can be spent on a range of goods as a face value voucher, with the GST on the sale when it is spent. Counting it as income when sold, and again when spent, counts the same sale twice.
  • Two shops. One combined entry loses which shop earned what and which shop's fees were paid. An entry per shop, each on its own job, keeps each shop's figures.

The automatic way, with PayKicker

PayKicker reads each Square shop's day every night and posts it into MYOB as one tax-inclusive sales invoice per shop, with the payments, every line on the shop's job. MYOB stays your books; PayKicker only reads from Square and never moves money.

  • Checked before it posts. Before a day posts, PayKicker checks it against Square: the lines must add up to Square's own order totals, the GST must equal Square's GST, and the payments must equal what the orders asked for. Every day is matched to Square's own totals to the cent. A day that does not add up is held with the reason and never posted.
  • Each piece on its own account. GST sales on GST and GST-free sales on FRE, card surcharges on their own income account, tips and gift cards sold on N-T to their own liability accounts, gift cards redeemed off the gift card liability, and discounts on their own line if your file keeps a discounts account.
  • Refunds on the day they were given, carrying the GST of the items refunded.
  • Card into Square clearing, cash into your cash account.
  • Each Square payout settled. What Square kept goes in as one spend money out of Square clearing: its fees on your fees account with their GST, and a Square Capital repayment or funds held on their own accounts with no GST. The payout is a transfer to your bank. The fees go on the shop's job too.
  • Nothing posts twice. PayKicker looks for each entry in MYOB before it writes it, so a retry never doubles, and a day already in MYOB (typed in, or posted by another app) is never posted again.
  • Sales by category, if you want it. Send a Square category, such as food or drinks, to its own income account; the invoice total and GST do not change.
  • Uber Eats and DoorDash too. Each weekly payout posts with the commission, fees, refunds, tips and GST on their own lines: Uber Eats and DoorDash payouts.

Questions

Can Square connect to MYOB?

Yes, through an app. MYOB's own help connects Square through a partner app rather than a built-in feed. PayKicker is one way: it posts each Square shop's day into MYOB every night, with each payout and Square's fees.

How do I record Square sales in MYOB by hand?

Once a day for each shop: one tax-inclusive entry with GST sales on the GST code, GST-free sales on FRE, card surcharges on their own income account, tips and gift cards sold on N-T to liability accounts, and refunds as negative lines. The card total goes into a Square clearing account and the cash into your cash account. When Square pays out, its fees come out of the clearing account and the deposit is a transfer to your bank.

Where do Square's fees go in MYOB?

On an expense account such as Square Fees, on the GST code, when Square pays out. Square takes its fees before it transfers the money, so the deposit is the card takings less the fees. Square gives you a monthly tax invoice for its fees in the Square Dashboard.

Can each shop have its own job in MYOB?

Yes. PayKicker posts each shop's day as its own sales invoice with every line on that shop's job, and puts the shop's Square fees on the same job, so each shop has its own figures in MYOB.

What if some days are already in MYOB?

Tell PayKicker when you set up. If another app or a bookkeeper has been entering your Square sales, it looks in MYOB for the last day already there, and you choose the first day it posts. A day or payout already in MYOB is never posted again.

How much does it cost?
