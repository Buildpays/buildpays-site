Square fees, tips, surcharges and GST in MYOB and Xero

A Square day is more than its sales. Tips, card surcharges, gift cards, refunds and Square's fees each have their own place in the books and their own GST treatment, and the payout that reaches your bank is none of them on its own. Here is where each piece goes in MYOB and Xero, with an example day worked through.

The rules behind it

Tips

The ATO's view is that a voluntary tip passed on to staff is not part of the price of the sale, so there is no GST on it, as long as you keep records showing the tips went to staff. A tip the business keeps, or a fixed amount charged as a service charge, is part of the price. Until tips are paid out they are money you owe your staff, which is why they sit in a liability account. How they reach your staff through payroll is a question for your accountant.

Card surcharges

The ATO treats a merchant's card surcharge as part of the price of what was sold. Its GST follows that sale: a surcharge on a taxable sale is taxable, a surcharge on a GST-free sale is GST-free. Keeping surcharges on their own income account shows what they bring in against what Square charges you.

Gift cards

A gift card that can be spent on a range of your goods is what the ATO calls a face value voucher. Selling one for its face value is not a taxable sale, so there is no GST when it is sold; GST is accounted for when the card is spent on taxable goods. Until then the card is a liability. If cards expire unused, ask your accountant: the ATO has a rule for that too.

Refunds

A refund for a returned sale is what the ATO calls an adjustment, generally reported in the period you become aware of it. So a refund belongs to the day it was given, not to the day of the original sale, and it reduces GST by the tax on what was refunded: none for a GST-free item.

Square's fees

Square charges its processing fees on the whole of each transaction, GST and tip included, takes them out before it transfers your money, and gives you a monthly GST tax invoice for them in the Square Dashboard under Account & Settings > Tax invoices. If you are registered for GST, the GST on the fees is a credit, and the ATO requires a tax invoice to claim a credit on a purchase over $82.50; Square's monthly invoice is that record. Check the claim with your accountant.

The payout

A payout is not income. It is money moving from the card balance Square holds for you to your bank, after Square has kept its fees and anything else it deducts, such as a Square Capital repayment. In the books it is a transfer out of a Square clearing account, and that account's balance is the card money Square has not paid out yet.

MYOB and Xero: the same entries in each one's words

In MYOB

The day is a tax-inclusive sales invoice to a customer for the shop, each line on the shop's job. Card and cash are customer payments into Square clearing (a Bank account) and your cash account. The payout is a spend money for what Square kept and a transfer money to your bank. MYOB's codes are GST, FRE (GST-free) and N-T (not reportable on the BAS).

In Xero

The day is a tax-inclusive sales invoice for the shop, each line on the shop's option in your jobs tracking category. Card and cash are payments into Square clearing (a BANK account) and your cash account. The payout is a spend money for what Square kept and a bank transfer to your bank. Xero's tax rates are GST on Income, GST Free Income, GST on Expenses, GST Free Expenses and BAS Excluded.

Questions

Is there GST on Square's fees?

Square gives you a monthly GST tax invoice for its processing fees in the Square Dashboard, on its Tax invoices page. If you are registered for GST, that invoice supports the GST credit on the fees; the ATO requires a tax invoice to claim a credit on a purchase over $82.50. Check the claim with your accountant.

Do I charge GST on tips?

Not on a voluntary tip that is passed on to your staff: the ATO's view is that it is not part of the price of the sale, as long as you keep records showing it went to staff. A tip the business keeps is part of the price and carries GST. PayKicker posts tips with no GST to a tips liability account.

Is a card surcharge subject to GST?

It follows the sale. The ATO treats a merchant's card surcharge as part of the price of what was sold, so a surcharge on a taxable sale carries GST and a surcharge on a GST-free sale does not.

When is GST paid on a gift card?

When it is spent, not when it is sold. The ATO treats a gift card that can be spent on a range of goods as a face value voucher: selling it for its face value is not a taxable sale, and GST is accounted for when it is redeemed for taxable goods. Until then it is a liability.

Which day does a refund belong to?

The day it was given. The ATO treats a refund for a returned sale as an adjustment, generally reported in the period you become aware of it, so last week's figures stay as they were and the refund reduces this period's GST by the tax on what was refunded.

Why doesn't my Square payout match my sales?

Because the payout is the card takings less Square's fees, and less any Square Capital repayment or funds Square holds, paid after the sale. Cash takings and gift card redemptions never pass through it.
